Finding north among the stars

How a spacecraft learns where it is

A star tracker photographs a small patch of sky and matches the pattern against a catalogue. With no magnetic compass to trust, the craft uses those distant lights to recover its orientation with remarkable precision.

  • The camera rejects bright stray light from the Sun and nearby surfaces.
  • Software compares angles between stars rather than relying on a single point.
  • A second sensor checks the answer before the craft turns.
